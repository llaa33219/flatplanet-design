FlatPlanet

01 — Foundations

The Rules

Five non-negotiable rules define every FlatPlanet component. Break one and the system stops feeling like itself.

36px Radius Ceiling

36 px is the maximum radius anywhere in the system. Smaller radii are permitted only when a short height forces it — a 36 px-tall control cannot carry a 36 px radius and still read as a control.

Zero Gradients

Gradients are banned outright. Depth comes from flat fills plus solid offset shadows — the geometry does the work, never the colour transition.

Hard Shadows

Main elements carry a solid offset shadow with zero blur, offset equally on both axes. Sub elements carry no resting shadow at all. On hover, the main shadow collapses to 2 px and the element presses down; sub elements lift and their shadow appears.

Three Speeds

0.1 s fast, 0.2 s default, 0.3 s slow. That is the entire timing vocabulary. Anything else is a bug.

BLP Colours Only

Every colour comes from the BLP palette. #fefeff for most surfaces, #000309 for dark, #007bff for accent.

Sub Elements Lift

When a parent hovers, its sub elements rise by the sub-shadow height and gain a hard shadow of their own — the depth hierarchy stays readable at every level. A rising element never loses its shadow.

02 — Elevation

Hard Shadows

Every shadow is a solid block of ink with zero blur. Main elements carry a hard shadow and no border — the shadow is their outline. Sub elements carry a real inline 4px border and no resting shadow. The ink is #00193d.

10 — Overlay

Modals, Drawers & Disclosure

Overlays use the same shell as cards — 36 px radius, hard shadow, solid scrim.

What is the 36px radius rule?
36 px is the ceiling for corner rounding across the entire system. Radii below 36 px are allowed only where the component's height makes a 36 px radius physically impossible to read — for example a 36 px-tall badge or a 28 px-tall chip.
How does the hover press work?
A main element's shadow starts at height H. On hover the shadow collapses to 2 px and the element descends by H − 2 px, so the shadow's absolute bottom edge never moves. Sub elements do the opposite: they rise by the sub-shadow height and gain that shadow at the same time.
Are gradients really banned?
Yes — no component uses a gradient anywhere. Depth is expressed purely with flat fills and solid offset shadows. The only gradient in the codebase is the skeleton shimmer, which is a loading affordance rather than a surface treatment.
